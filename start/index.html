YOUR FIRST DAY

Your first day starts here.

You do not need tournament experience, perfect gear, or years of LARP knowledge to begin. Come prepared to listen, move safely, ask questions, and try.

IS THE ACADEMY FOR ME?

If you want to learn deliberately, there is a place to begin.

Structured training is useful whether you are holding a foam sword for the first time or trying to break through a plateau after years of play.

I have never foam fought before.

Good. Beginners can start with safety, movement, simple mechanics, and controlled partner work.

I already play Amtgard.

The Academy can give familiar practice more structure through focused goals, drills, and post-fight analysis.

I want to improve a weapon style.

Weapon Arts develops practical literacy in single sword, shield, florentine, long weapons, backup weapons, and projectile awareness.

I want to think better in a fight.

Combat Intelligence focuses on range, tempo, reads, baits, feints, planning, adaptation, and pressure management.

I care more about battlegames than tournaments.

Field and Team Combat trains lines, objectives, communication, specialists, movement, and useful team decisions.

I may want to teach someday.

The Academy treats teaching and leadership as skills: explain clearly, protect enthusiasm, give useful feedback, and build independence.

WHAT HAPPENS THE FIRST TIME?

A first visit should feel like a doorway, not an exam.

The exact park day can vary, but a good Academy first experience follows a simple shape.

Welcome & orientation

Meet the people running the field, learn the boundaries, and understand what is happening that day.

Safety & gear

Learn HOLD, basic field conduct, and how equipment checks or loaner arrangements work locally.

One focused lesson

Learn something manageable and useful instead of being flooded with every rule and technique at once.

Guided practice

Drill the idea with a partner or instructor at a speed appropriate to your experience.

Field application

When appropriate, try the skill in controlled sparring, ditch, or a simple game problem.

Review

Leave knowing one thing you learned and one thing worth practicing next time.

WHAT TO BRING

Keep the first visit simple.

  • Comfortable clothes you can move in.
  • Athletic footwear appropriate for an outdoor field.
  • Water—more than you think you will need in warm weather.
  • Sun, cold, or weather protection appropriate to the day.
  • A willingness to listen, ask questions, and start slow.

ABOUT EQUIPMENT

Do not buy a pile of gear just to discover whether you enjoy Amtgard.

Amtgard encourages new players to contact the local group before visiting and notes that chapters commonly help newcomers get started. Local equipment availability can vary, so confirm what is available before you travel.

The Academy does not promise loaner weapons or protective equipment on every date unless the local organizer has confirmed it.

SAFETY COMES FIRST

The first combat skill is control.

  • HOLD means stop immediately. Freeze movement and weapon action, then look for the problem.
  • Use controlled movement and controlled shots. Foam is safer when the person wielding it is disciplined.
  • Do not deliberately target unsafe areas.
  • Match intensity to the people training around you—especially beginners.
  • Official field rulings belong to the proper field authority for the day.

FIRST-DAY FAQ

Remove uncertainty before you arrive.

Do I need experience?

No. The Academy is built around progressive instruction. A new fighter begins with safety, clear basics, and appropriate pressure rather than being expected to already know how to fight.

Do I need my own weapon?

Do not assume you need to purchase gear before your first visit. Contact the local group first and ask what equipment is available that day. Availability can vary.

Is the Academy the same thing as Amtgard?

No. The Academy is an Amtgard-focused training program. Amtgard, Dragonspine, the local chapter, and proper field officials retain their normal authority over the game, rules, records, and field.

Do I have to care about tournaments?

No. Some fighters enjoy competition, but Academy training also supports battlegames, team combat, movement, class play, field awareness, teaching, and long-term personal improvement.

What if I am nervous about fighting?

That is a reasonable way to feel. Training can begin with explanation, movement without strikes, cooperative drills, and slow controlled work before pressure increases.

Are there waivers or age requirements?

Yes, Amtgard uses membership/waiver requirements, and minors require guardian involvement. Exact local participation requirements should be confirmed with Obsidian Gate and current Dragonspine/Amtgard guidance before taking the field.

What if I just want to watch first?

That is a good way to learn the shape of the field. Check with the local organizers, introduce yourself, and tell them you are interested in the Academy or Amtgard but want to observe before jumping in.

COME LEARN THE FIRST GATE

You can become better after you arrive. You only need to arrive willing to learn.

Check the current local plan, introduce yourself, and tell someone you are new. The first victory is simply beginning well.
