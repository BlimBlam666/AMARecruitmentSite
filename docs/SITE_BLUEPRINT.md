# AMA Recruitment Site — Site Blueprint

**Repository:** `BlimBlam666/AMARecruitmentSite`  
**Phase:** Design blueprint — no production site code yet  
**Primary purpose:** Recruitment + general public information  
**Working principle:** The site is the Academy's **front gate**, not its command center.

---

## 1. Product Definition

The Academy of Mercenary Arts recruitment site should answer five newcomer questions quickly:

1. **What is this?**
2. **Is this for someone like me?**
3. **What will I learn and experience?**
4. **Is it safe, organized, and welcoming?**
5. **How do I start?**

A visitor should understand the Academy within roughly 30 seconds and reach a useful next step without needing to understand internal Academy terminology first.

### Primary conversion

**Start Here / Come Train With Us**

The site should move an interested visitor toward attending, contacting the Academy, or following the current official information source for the next training opportunity.

### Secondary conversions

- Explore the training path.
- Learn how the Academy relates to Amtgard and Dragonspine.
- Follow Academy social channels.
- View Academy videos/photos.
- Reach official Amtgard/Dragonspine/local-park resources.

### Explicit non-goals

The public site should **not** become:

- the Academy Command Hall,
- an internal operations dashboard,
- a full searchable course database,
- a replacement for Amtgard's Rules of Play,
- a replacement for Dragonspine Corpora,
- a rules-adjudication site,
- an events CMS,
- a member records system,
- a blog that requires frequent maintenance,
- or a social feed mirror.

---

## 2. Audience

### Primary audience — Curious newcomer

Someone sees the Academy at a park, convention, social post, video, QR code, or through a friend. They may know nothing about Amtgard or LARP.

They need:
- plain language,
- compelling visuals,
- reassurance,
- simple expectations,
- and a clear next step.

### Secondary audience — Existing Amtgard player

They already understand foam fighting but want to know what makes the Academy different.

They need:
- the training philosophy,
- the progression system,
- representative courses,
- the Academy's standards,
- and pathways for deeper development.

### Tertiary audience — Parent, friend, organizer, or community partner

They may not intend to fight but want to understand whether the Academy is responsible and legitimate.

They need:
- safety-first language,
- relationship to official Amtgard authority,
- public contact information,
- and a concise explanation of the program.

### C. The Academy Standard

**Heading:**  
## What We Value on the Field

Feature the five Cadet habits:

**Safety** — protect people before chasing victory.  
**Honor** — call your own shots and take your own deaths.

**Attention** — notice what is happening before rushing to act.

### D. Cadets and Preceptors

Two-column layout.

**Cadets**  
Students of the Academy. Beginners are not expected to be excellent; they are expected to be safe, honest, teachable, and willing to practice.

**Preceptors**  
Academy instructors and field teachers. Their job is not to demonstrate superiority, but to make learning possible, give useful feedback, protect enthusiasm, and model the standard they teach.

Do not turn this into a staff directory in v1.

# 7. START HERE PAGE

Remove uncertainty. This is the most important conversion page.

## Wireframe

### A. Hero

**Heading:**  
# Your First Day Starts Here.

**Intro:**  
You do not need tournament experience, perfect gear, or years of LARP knowledge to start learning. Come prepared to listen, move safely, ask questions, and try.

---

### B. Is the Academy for Me?

Use simple yes-oriented cases:

- I have never foam fought before.
- I already play Amtgard and want structured practice.
- I want to improve one weapon style.
- I want to become better at team combat.
- I want to understand *why* techniques work.
- I may want to teach or lead someday.

Avoid claims about age eligibility until current local/Kingdom requirements and waiver language are verified for the page.

---

### C. What Happens the First Time?

Recommended sequence:

1. **Welcome & orientation**  
   Learn who is running the field and what is happening that day.

2. **Safety / gear check**  
   Understand boundaries, equipment expectations, HOLD, and basic field conduct.

3. **One focused lesson**  
   Learn a manageable skill rather than being flooded with theory.

4. **Guided practice**  
   Drill it with a partner or instructor at appropriate speed.

5. **Optional field application**  
   Use the lesson in controlled sparring, ditch, or a simple game when appropriate.

6. **Review**  
   Leave with one thing learned and one thing to practice next.

Do not promise every first visit will follow the identical schedule; present this as the expected Academy experience.

---

### D. What to Bring

Only publish confirmed universal needs:

- comfortable clothes you can move in,
- appropriate athletic footwear for the field,
- water,
- weather protection,
- willingness to listen and learn.

Equipment wording:

**Weapons / protective gear:**  
State current loaner availability only after it has been confirmed. Do not promise equipment that may not be available on a particular day.

---

### E. Safety Comes First

Brief, prominent block:

- HOLD means stop immediately.
- Controlled movement and controlled shots matter.
- Do not target unsafe areas.
- Match intensity to the people training with you.
- Official field rulings belong to the day's proper field authority.

Link to current Rules of Play.

---

### F. Where and When

This should be a single canonical section on the site.

Required before launch:
- verified current practice location,
- verified current training day/time,
- map/location link,
- who to contact before attending,
- local park link,
- weather/cancellation source if applicable.

**Maintenance rule:**  
If schedule information changes often, prefer linking to the current official calendar/social source rather than duplicating stale event listings across the site.

Do **not** put an Academy launch date on the site.

---

### G. First-Day FAQ

Recommended v1 questions:

**Do I need experience?**  
No. Training should meet the fighter where they are.

**Do I need my own weapon?**  
Answer only after current loaner policy is confirmed.

**Is this the same as Amtgard?**  
The Academy is an Amtgard-focused training program; Amtgard and its proper officials remain the authority for the game.

**Do I have to be competitive?**  
No. Structured training can serve recreation, battlegame skill, tournament goals, teamwork, or long-term mastery.

**What if I am nervous about fighting?**  
New participants should be able to begin with explanation, slow drills, and controlled practice before pressure increases.

**Are there age or waiver requirements?**  
Link to current official local/Dragonspine requirements rather than paraphrasing them unless they are verified at launch.

# 9. VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM

## Overall mood

**A real medieval martial academy interpreted through a clean modern website.**

Not:
- fantasy-game HUD,
- faux parchment everywhere,
- noisy RPG inventory interface,
- generic corporate landing page,
- grimdark brutality.

Desired qualities:
- disciplined,
- welcoming,
- storied,
- physical,
- handcrafted,
- credible,
- readable.

## Suggested palette

Use the existing Academy character:

- **Obsidian / charcoal** — primary dark background
- **Warm parchment / bone** — reading surfaces and light text
- **Antique brass / muted gold** — highlights and calls to action
- **Thistle purple** — restrained identity accent
- **Iron gray** — borders and secondary structure

Exact values should be finalized against the current crest/logo so the digital palette matches the heraldry rather than inventing a competing identity.

## Typography

Recommended direction:

- Display/headings: a restrained classical or inscription-inspired serif.
- Body/UI: highly readable humanist sans-serif or clean serif.
- Never use decorative medieval fonts for paragraphs, buttons, FAQ answers, or long text.

Typography hierarchy must remain readable on a phone in sunlight.

## Decorative language

Use sparingly:
- thin heraldic borders,
- shield/book/sword line motifs,
- subtle iron or parchment texture,
- engraved dividers,
- small thistle details.

Avoid large textured backgrounds behind body copy.

## Priority order

1. Real Academy training photographs.
2. Real Amtgard field photographs with permission.
3. Academy heraldry / crest.
4. Purpose-built simple illustrations or icons.
5. Stock imagery only as a temporary development placeholder.

## Desired photo set

Before launch, ideally collect:

- hero image — instruction/training in motion,
- beginner or paired drill,
- single-sword / weapon training,
- group/team field scene,
- Preceptor teaching a small group,
- candid community moment,
- optional Graduate Trial / tournament image.

## Photo rules

- Obtain appropriate permission before publishing identifiable people.
- Favor genuine training over posed “tough fighter” portraits.
- Show diversity of experience and roles where possible.
- Avoid photographs that visually suggest unsafe targeting or uncontrolled contact.
- Write meaningful alt text for informational images.
- Decorative images should not create screen-reader noise.

# 11. MOBILE-FIRST BEHAVIOR

The majority of QR/social traffic may arrive by phone, so mobile is the primary layout, not a reduced desktop layout.

Requirements:

- readable at 320px+ widths,
- no horizontal scrolling,
- large tap targets,
- sticky or easily reachable navigation,
- Start Here CTA visible without hunting,
- tier path becomes vertical,
- cards become single column,
- FAQ uses accessible disclosure elements if collapsed,
- images crop safely,
- no autoplay audio,
- optional video must not block understanding,
- honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 12. ACCESSIBILITY STANDARD

Target WCAG 2.2 AA practices for v1:

- semantic headings in correct order,
- keyboard-operable navigation,
- visible focus styles,
- sufficient color contrast,
- text alternatives for meaningful images,
- labels for controls,
- no information conveyed by color alone,
- reduced-motion support,
- legible font sizing / line height,
- links that explain their destination,
- no hover-only content.

Accessibility is part of the Academy's welcoming standard, not a post-launch patch.

---

# 13. CONTENT GOVERNANCE

## Public-copy rules

Every page should follow these rules:

1. **Plain language first; Academy language second.**
2. Explain Amtgard terms before relying on them.
3. Never imply the Academy has official rules authority it does not possess.
4. Link to official current sources for rules, waivers, membership, and field authority.
5. Never publish a hard launch date unless one truly exists.
6. Do not publish internal planning notes, private member information, AARs, drafts, or operations.
7. Do not promise loaner gear, instructors, classes, dates, or attendance conditions unless verified.
8. Prefer evergreen copy over material that requires weekly edits.
9. Social media tells people **what is happening now**; this website tells people **what the Academy is and how to join it**.
10. Command Hall tells members **how to run the Academy**; the recruitment site tells the public **why they might want to enter it**.

# 19. V1 DESIGN CONTRACT

If a proposed feature does not help a newcomer understand the Academy, trust it, or take the next step, it probably does not belong in v1.

The site should feel like **standing at the Academy gate and being invited inside**.

The Command Hall is for those already doing the work.

This site is for the person who has just discovered that the work exists.
